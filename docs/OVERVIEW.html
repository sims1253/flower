Flower: teaching memory to a very small language model

A from-scratch research framework for memory-augmented attention at the 12–26M-parameter scale. Nine sweeps in, the central question has shifted from "which memory mechanism wins?" to "how do we even measure whether memory is doing anything?" — and that turned out to be the more interesting problem.

01 What this project is

Flower is a single-researcher language-model lab. The premise: at ~25M parameters you can run dozens of controlled experiments on one GPU in a weekend, so it’s the right place to ask whether external memory mechanisms — a small bank of summary slots that survives across a long context — actually help a transformer, and if so, which kind.

Every model is a tiny decoder-only transformer (4–6 layers, d_model 256–384) with a local attention window (64–128 tokens) plus a pluggable memory module. The memory is the variable under study: hierarchical max-pool summaries, Bloom-hash routing, optimal-transport couplings, energy-based reads, frequency-decay slots, and more. Everything else (data, tokenizer, optimizer, training length) is frozen per sweep so that differences are attributable.

The actual end goal

The small-model work is a microscope, not the product. The north star, laid out in the research brief and roadmap, is a personal multilingual assistant: continued-pretrain a strong open base (Qwen3-4B-Base or Gemma-3-4B) on German + English, then QLoRA + DoRA finetune on personal chat history. Sweeps 7–10 exist to decide whether any memory mechanism is even worth porting into that run — and just as importantly, to give the writeup its spine: how we learned to measure, and what actually moved the needle.

Why small-scale at all? The reports converge on a blunt prior: at 1B+, data quality is 3–5× more impactful than architecture. So the only way architecture questions are worth asking is at a scale where you can afford to ask them rigorously — with seeds, gates, and ablations rather than a single loss number.

02 What has been tried

The project runs as a sequence of numbered sweeps, each changing one axis at a time. Two parallel tracks: the main memory-mechanism track (Sweeps 1–9) and a newer KV-cache compaction track reproducing the "Still" paper.

Sweep 1 — the first signalMay 2026
Hierarchical max-pooled memory appeared to give a 2.6× PPL win over vanilla local attention. Later proven partly illusory — the headline "PPL 119.5" was train PPL on a silently-broken validation split. But the architecture was real and became the running baseline (summary_memory / hier_max_16).
Sweeps 2–3 — fixing the measurementMay 2026
Two bugs found and fixed: the LR scheduler used constant because warmup_steps was never wired into the trainer, and the validation split was leaky. Also swapped the GPT-2 50k tokenizer (80% of params in the embedding!) for a 4K BPE trained on FineWeb-Edu, putting embedding fraction near the scaling-law-optimal ~25%. Real, honest result after fixes: val PPL 12.69 on a properly held-out split, 25.8M-param hier-max-16 model.
Sweep 4 — the optimizer is the headlineMay 2026
Phase 0 swept muon_lr × ns_steps × warmup and found the recipe that became the project default: muon@lr3e-3 / ns5 / warm1k → val 3.413. Phase 1 retrofitted RoPE and bumped training to seq=2048 so memory slots actually have something to compress. This sweep quietly set up the biggest methodological lesson of the project (see Sweep 7).
Sweep 5 — novel memory bake-offMay 2026
Four original mechanisms vs the hier-max control: bloom_memory, flow_ot_memory, frequency_decay_memory, surprise_memory. Winner: bloom_memory val 3.594 vs control 3.627 (~2% BPB). But the margin was inside what later turned out to be a recipe confound, and flow_ot's Sinkhorn plan collapsed to uniform (= mean-pooling, bought nothing).
Sweep 6 — symplectic Q/K attention (clean negative)May 2026
Replaced the query/key projection with a SympNet-style Hamiltonian flow (volume-preserving, bounded energy drift). Result: every flow variant lost badly to plain local attention (vanilla val 3.792; flow variants 4.4–4.8). Energy drift stayed bounded as the theory promised, but the mechanism was pure overhead at this scale. The whole Q/K-flow direction was falsified.
Sweep 7 — “fix the lens, then bet”May–Jun 2026
The pivotal sweep. Diagnosis: the reason all mechanisms tied was mostly a measurement problem. Memory is redundant at seq=2048 with window=128; the recall probes were too easy; Sweep 5/6 ran on the wrong (AdamW) recipe; and there were no seeds. Sweep 7 rebuilt the eval suite: a capacity-curve MQAR probe, multi-seed, and a discrimination gate. Two mechanism bets followed — load-bearing memory (B1) and the energy-based read (B2), which became the first mechanism to show a real, transferable effect.
Still track — KV-cache compaction, in parallelJun 2026
A separate reproduction of the Still paper (arXiv:2606.07878): a per-layer Perceiver trained once to compress the KV cache in a single forward pass via KL distillation. Implemented at ~2M-param scale with four novel variants (OT read, energy read, frequency decay, spectral). Latest 5-seed run: baseline and freq-decay compactor statistically tied (~2.82 PPL) — the question is open but the infrastructure is built.
A note on rigor Every sweep since Sweep 7 is gated: a probe must demonstrably separate known variants across seeds before any new architecture runs on it. No ruler, no experiment. This is the single biggest cultural shift in the project.

03 How the things tried compare

Three honest comparisons. The headline across all of them: architecture/mechanism changes move val loss by ≤0.03 bpb at this scale — smaller than the optimizer choice and barely above seed noise. The discriminating signal lives in the probes, not the loss.

3.3 — The one mechanism that transferred: energy-based read (β=0.25)

Sweep 7 B2 replaced the convex softmax mean-pool memory read with a log-sum-exp energy read controlled by a learnable inverse temperature β. On the load-bearing long-context regime (seq 2048, window 64, eval 8192), it became the current winner — not by raising the ceiling, but by removing the bad-seed collapse.

04 What we actually learned

Most of the value so far is methodological, not architectural. Five findings that reshaped the project:

lesson The null result was a ruler problem

Sweeps 1–6 produced a consistent "nothing moves val loss." The fix wasn't a better model — it was a probe that puts memory under pressure (context » window) and reports a capacity curve, not a point.

lesson Optimizer > architecture at this scale

The Muon-vs-AdamW recipe gap (~0.2 nats) dwarfed every architecture effect (~0.03 bpb). Comparing mechanisms on a badly-tuned optimizer is measuring noise. LR matters more than optimizer family, too.

lesson Memory must be load-bearing to matter

At seq=2048 / window=128, local attention already covers the context — memory is decorative. Mechanisms only separate when train and eval context » window, forcing cross-window dependencies through memory.

lesson On-manifold > synthetic probes

Zero-shot synthetic MQAR floors at 0 on FineWeb-trained models — the task layout is off-manifold, not just the tokens. memory_ablation_delta and needle_in_text are the trustworthy FineWeb rulers.

lesson Stability beats ceiling

The energy read's win was never a higher peak — it removed the bad-seed collapse (needle BP 6.7±1.9 → 8.0±0.0). Robustness across seeds may be the more publishable, more honest mechanism signal.

lesson Vary one axis at a time

Data, optimizer, parameterization, and precision are each deliberately deferred to their own sweep. Bundling them destroys attribution — the project's roadmap is built around this discipline.

05 The parallel track: KV-cache compaction (Still)

Alongside the memory-mechanism sweeps, the project reproduces and extends Still: Amortized KV Cache Compaction in a Single Forward Pass (arXiv:2606.07878). A small per-layer Perceiver is trained once against a frozen base model; at inference it compresses the full per-layer KV cache into compact latents.

What's implemented

  • Core (still.py): Perceiver cross-attention + self-attention compactor with QK-norm, position-free compaction (inverse-RoPE → re-RoPE), and identity-style init.
  • Training wrapper (still_lm.py): frozen base + per-layer compactors, teacher (full cache) / student (compact cache) passes, top-k KL distillation.
  • Four novel variants: OT-coupled read, energy-sharpened read, frequency-decay slots, and spectral — plus a full-stack combination.
  • Compaction-specific probes (still_eval.py): compaction-KL curve, needle-through-compaction, iterative-compaction sweep, compression-utility frontier.

At this scale the novel variants are statistically indistinguishable from baseline — the same "ruler isn't sharp enough yet" pattern as the main track. Open question: do OT/energy/spectral separate under the iterative-compaction probe, where baseline is expected to degrade?

Scale caveat The paper uses Qwen3-4B at 8k–128k context on 8×H200. Flower trains compactors on ~2M-param bases at seq 512–2048. The goal is not to match paper numbers — it's to verify the architecture works and to test whether the novel variants improve on baseline at a scale where iteration is cheap.

06 What's open & on the horizon

The roadmap is deliberately ordered: trust the ruler, then vary one big lever at a time, then scale. Each deferred axis becomes its own sweep so attribution stays clean.

Optimizer (LR-controlled)

Muon vs AdamW vs Aurora, each at its own best LR (per-optimizer LR sweep first). Aurora claims to fix Muon's MLP neuron death; reality-check says no optimizer beats ~1.4× a well-tuned AdamW. Honest bar.

IsoFLOP ladder + CompleteP

The question that started all this: does the mechanism gap open with scale? Run the winning mechanism at 25M / 70M / 160M under CompleteP parameterization. Go/no-go for committing to a large run.

axis: scale + parameterization
Horizon · the goal

Personal multilingual assistant

Don't pretrain 1–7B from scratch. Continued-pretrain Qwen3-4B-Base or Gemma-3-4B on DE+EN, then QLoRA+DoRA finetune on personal chat history. Sweeps 7–10 decide whether any mechanism is worth porting in.

axis: deployment
The open question everything hinges on Across nine sweeps, no mechanism has beaten the no-memory control by more than seed noise on language-modeling loss. The energy read is a real but modest robustness effect, not a capacity breakthrough. Sweep 10's scale ladder is the decisive test: if the gap stays flat at 70M/160M, the honest conclusion is that external memory isn't worth the complexity at this regime — and the project's contribution becomes the methodology (how to measure memory) rather than a winning architecture. Either outcome is a publishable, honest result.
